Summer

Summer is for eating grapes by the fistful, like popcorn. They are carrying my summer on their stems, shriveling under the weight of their own sweetness.

I feel my contacts becoming plastic again in my eye, my carriage turning back into pumpkins. I rub them, pulling my makeup into my tear ducts, making the problem worse. My skin is swollen with sun, evaporating my sweat along with whatever’s left of the sunscreen I applied too many hours ago. There isn’t a lick of wind, but the sun will set soon. I hope to dull my awareness of how heavy each of my limbs feels, so I can enjoy the evening, like the young person at heart I’m pretending to be.

Your eyes brush over mine, and I feel the sun blare one last beat before it ticks behind the horizon. Our wavelengths catch each other in unison, but your gaze moves past me, and I move past myself to pretend I’m paying attention to the conversation I’m supposed to be participating in.

Eventually we find each other, or maybe I just find you. I smile with my teeth a little too exposed. It feels so electric, so magnetic, so sore. Feeling intensely like a teenager. Yearning is for being fourteen years old. Yearning is for summer. Maybe I’m not pretending to be young at heart after all.

“Do you write? You always say such lovely things.” He looks down and smiles and turns a little red. He doesn’t answer, and turns the focus back to me. The attention between us feels alive, engulfing. I could wrap my arms around it to hold its solidity, and I want to. Intoxicated by his collar bone, I miss what he said. I observe him as he takes in the sun reflecting off the blues of my eyes and feel him pull into them.
